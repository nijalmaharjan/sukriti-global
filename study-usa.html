About Education in the United States

The United States is one of the world's leading study destinations, offering unmatched academic diversity, cutting-edge research opportunities, and globally recognized degrees. With thousands of accredited institutions and flexible academic pathways, the USA attracts Nepali students seeking innovation, career growth, and long-term professional success.

Graduates from U.S. universities are highly valued worldwide for their practical skills, industry exposure, and global perspective. The U.S. education system is known for its flexibility and student-centered approach — students can choose from community colleges, universities, and research institutions, with the option to transfer between institutions.

Why Choose the United States?

  • Home to the largest number of top-ranked universities in the world
  • Wide range of programs and specializations across various disciplines
  • Strong emphasis on research, innovation, and practical learning
  • Opportunities for paid internships and on-campus employment
  • Post-study work opportunities through Optional Practical Training (OPT)

Requirements

Key Requirements

Academic Qualification

Minimum GPA 2.8 (or equivalent) for bachelor's and master's programs. Study gap of 2–5 years accepted for master's with valid justification.

  • SEE + Grade 12 with minimum GPA 2.8 for undergraduate programs
  • Bachelor's degree (minimum 50%) required for postgraduate programs
  • Study gap up to 5 years accepted for master's with proper documentation
  • Notarized/attested transcripts and degree certificates required
  • NEB equivalency required for foreign board certificates

English Proficiency

IELTS 6.0 for bachelor's / IELTS 6.5 for master's. TOEFL iBT 61+ (bachelor's) / 80+ (master's) also accepted.

  • IELTS: minimum 6.0 for undergraduate, 6.5 for postgraduate
  • TOEFL iBT: minimum 61 for bachelor's, 80 for master's
  • PTE Academic: minimum 50 for bachelor's, 58 for master's
  • Duolingo English Test accepted by select universities (85+)
  • Test scores must be valid (within 2 years of application date)

Financial Proof

Proof of funds covering tuition and living costs. Bank statements from family income, salary, property rental, or business income accepted.

  • Community College / Associate: approx. NPR 30–45 Lakhs
  • Bachelor's programs: approx. NPR 45–65 Lakhs
  • Master's programs: approx. NPR 40–60 Lakhs
  • Income sources: salary, house rent, land lease, business documents
  • Education loan sanction letter also accepted as proof of funds

F-1 Visa Documentation

Valid passport, I-20 form from a SEVP-certified institution, SEVIS registration fee payment, and DS-160 visa application required.

  • Valid passport (minimum 6 months beyond intended stay)
  • I-20 form issued by SEVP-approved U.S. institution
  • SEVIS fee payment receipt (Form I-901)
  • DS-160 online visa application and visa interview appointment
  • SOP, 2–3 LORs, academic transcripts, and financial documents

Career & Work

Work & Career Opportunities

The USA offers strong work and career pathways for international students during and after their studies.

On-Campus Employment (F-1)

F-1 students may work on campus for up to 20 hours per week during academic sessions and full-time during official vacation periods. On-campus jobs include library, cafeteria, research assistant, and administrative roles.

Optional Practical Training (OPT)

After graduation, F-1 students are eligible for 12 months of OPT — paid work authorization in a field related to your degree. No employer sponsorship needed during OPT period. Applications submitted 90 days before graduation.

STEM OPT Extension

Graduates from STEM-designated programs (CS, Data Science, Engineering, etc.) qualify for a 24-month STEM OPT extension — totaling up to 3 years of post-study work authorization in the USA.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need IELTS or TOEFL to study in the USA? +

Yes, most U.S. universities require proof of English proficiency. IELTS, TOEFL iBT, and PTE Academic are the most widely accepted. Some universities also accept the Duolingo English Test. Minimum scores vary by institution and program level.

How long does the F-1 student visa process take? +

The F-1 visa process typically takes 4–8 weeks from the time you receive your I-20 form. It's recommended to begin the process at least 3 months before your program start date to account for document preparation and visa interview scheduling.

Can I work while studying in the USA on an F-1 visa? +

Yes. F-1 students are allowed to work on campus for up to 20 hours per week during the academic semester. Off-campus work is allowed through Curricular Practical Training (CPT) and Optional Practical Training (OPT) after completing at least one academic year.

What is OPT and how does it work for Nepali graduates? +

Optional Practical Training (OPT) allows F-1 graduates to work in the USA for up to 12 months in a field related to their degree. STEM graduates are eligible for a 24-month extension, giving them up to 3 years total. Many Nepali students use OPT as a pathway to H-1B visa sponsorship.

What is the total cost of studying in the USA for Nepali students? +

Tuition fees vary widely: community colleges USD $6,000–15,000/year; state universities USD $15,000–30,000/year; private universities USD $30,000–60,000+/year. Living costs typically add USD $12,000–25,000/year depending on location. Scholarships and assistantships can significantly reduce these costs.

Can I bring my family with me on an F-1 student visa? +

Yes. Your spouse and unmarried children under 21 can accompany you on an F-2 dependent visa. However, F-2 visa holders are not permitted to work in the USA. F-2 dependents may study part-time but cannot pursue a full-time degree program.
